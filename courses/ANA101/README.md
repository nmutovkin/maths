# ANA101 Calculus I: Single Variable

Limits, derivatives, integrals and series for functions of one real variable.
First course of the [analysis track](../../curriculum/02-analysis.md).

## About this course

**Prerequisites.** Secondary-school algebra. Unit 0 reviews everything else
the course uses (inequalities, functions, exponentials, logarithms and
trigonometry). Like the rest of the series, the course assumes you can read
and follow proofs.

**Level of rigour.** The main theorems are proved, using the precise
(ε–δ) definition of a limit. A few results depend on the completeness of the
real numbers: the intermediate value theorem, the extreme value theorem,
integrability of continuous functions, and the monotone convergence theorem.
These are stated precisely and explained intuitively here; their full proofs
are given in ANA202 Real Analysis I.

**After the course** you should be able to compute limits, derivatives and
integrals fluently, use them to solve problems in geometry and physics, and
decide whether series converge. The milestone task is: find the Taylor series
of a function such as ln(1 + x), give its radius of convergence, and bound the
error of a Taylor polynomial on an interval.

**Optional reading.** Stewart, *Calculus: Early Transcendentals*; Spivak,
*Calculus*; Apostol, *Calculus* Vol. 1.

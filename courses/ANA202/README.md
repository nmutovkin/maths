# ANA202 Real Analysis I

Single-variable calculus rebuilt from the axioms of the real numbers, with
every result proved. Part of the
[analysis track](../../curriculum/02-analysis.md).

## About this course

**Prerequisites.** [ANA101 Calculus I](../ANA101/README.md). You should be
comfortable reading proofs. This is the first course of the series in which
almost every exercise asks you to write one, and Unit 0 sets out the logical
tools this needs.

**Level of rigour.** Everything is proved from the axioms of a complete
ordered field. The one exception is the optional Lesson 1.5, which sketches
how such a field can be constructed from the rational numbers.

**What this course completes from ANA101.** ANA101 stated some theorems that
depend on completeness of the real numbers without proving them. They are
proved here:

| Result | Stated in ANA101 | Proved here |
|---|---|---|
| Monotone convergence theorem | Lesson 7.1 | Lesson 2.3 |
| Extreme value theorem | Lesson 3.1 | Lesson 5.3 |
| Intermediate value theorem | Lesson 1.6 | Lesson 5.4 |
| Continuous functions are integrable | Lesson 4.2 | Lesson 7.2 |

ANA101 also used the exponential, logarithm and trigonometric functions with
their familiar properties. Lesson 8.5 defines them by power series and proves
those properties.

**Writing proofs.** Every exercise solution is written as a complete proof
that can serve as a model. When you check your work, compare the structure of
the argument with the model, not just the final answer. A different correct
proof is still correct.

**After the course** you should be able to prove the standard theorems of
single-variable calculus and work fluently with ε–δ and ε–N arguments,
sequences, compactness and uniform convergence. The milestone task is: prove
that a uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, and give an
example showing that pointwise convergence is not enough.

**Optional reading.** Abbott, *Understanding Analysis*; Rudin, *Principles of
Mathematical Analysis*; Tao, *Analysis I*.

## Connections to other courses

- **Uses from ANA101:** the computational side of calculus, so that examples
  and counterexamples can be checked quickly. Nothing from ANA101 is assumed
  as a proved fact.
- **Prepares for:** ANA203 Real Analysis II (metric spaces generalize Unit 4;
  Unit 8 continues with Arzelà–Ascoli and Stone–Weierstrass), ANA301 Complex
  Analysis, ANA303 Fourier Analysis, GEO301 Point-Set Topology, PRB301
  Mathematical Statistics.
- **Overlap with ANA201:** ANA201 introduces uniform convergence only as far
  as Picard iteration needs it. Unit 8 here gives the full treatment.

## Open questions

1. **Lesson 1.5 (constructing ℝ).** Keep it as an optional lesson, move it to
   an appendix, or drop it.
2. **Unit 0.** Since the series assumes proof skills, Unit 0 is a short
   refresher. It could be cut if you think it is unnecessary, or expanded if
   learners arrive with weaker proof skills than assumed.
